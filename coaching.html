The coaching partnership

Co-Head Coach Model

Bananas 2K15 is built around a co-head coaching partnership between Brian Rogers and Bob. Both are seasoned coaches. Both have daughters playing high school softball. Both understand what it means to be a coach and a dad at the same time.

The partnership is intentional. When Brian needs to be in the stands watching his daughter's high school game, Bob leads practice. When Bob has a conflict, Brian leads. The players always have a head coach present — and both coaches are always present in the ways that matter most to their own families.

Family first is not an excuse — it's the policy. This org was built by parents who understood that the coaches showing up for their own kids is exactly the kind of character we want coaching ours. A coach who prioritizes family is a coach who teaches the right values on the field.

How it works in practice

The Coverage Model

Both coaches hold equal authority on the field. There is no primary and backup — there are two head coaches who communicate, plan together, and trust each other to lead. The org facilitates this through documented expectations, not informal arrangements.

Schedule communicated to Co-Directors monthly
Both coaches share any known conflicts at the start of each month. The org plans around it — no last-minute scrambles, no players left without leadership.
One co-head is always present at every game
Tournaments and official games always have at least one co-head coach present. If a scheduling conflict affects game coverage, it is flagged to the Co-Directors with at least two weeks' notice.
Shared practice plans — no institutional knowledge gap
Both coaches operate from the same development framework. When one is absent, practice continues on plan — not improvised. Players experience consistency regardless of which coach is on the field.
Family conflicts are never criticized — they're expected
The org explicitly supports both coaches prioritizing their daughters' high school seasons. This expectation is communicated to all families at the start of the season so there's no ambiguity when it happens.
No parent may pressure either coach about family absences
The family-first policy is a Board-level commitment. Any parent who challenges a coach's absence for family reasons is redirected to the Co-Directors. This is non-negotiable.
Shared philosophy

How Brian & Bob Coach

Athlete First

Character, effort, and coachability before stats. A player's development as a person matters as much as her development as an athlete.

Technical Foundation

Mechanics taught correctly from the start. Bad habits at 10U become ceilings at 14. We do the work now so the player can go further later.

Long-Term Thinking

We develop players who are still competing at 16 and 18 because they were built right — not burned out winning one tournament banner.

Family Builds Winners

A coach who shows up for his own daughter's game is modeling the same priority he teaches on the field. Family first is part of the curriculum.

Coach protection

The Firewall Policy

Both co-head coaches are protected by the same firewall. No parent, Board Member, or Co-Director has authority over coaching decisions — and no one criticizes family-first absences.

Coaching concerns → addressed by the relevant co-head coach, face-to-face, within 24 hours of the game
Org concerns (dues, scheduling, conduct) → Co-Directors only, never the coaches
Family absences by either coach are communicated in advance, covered by the other, and never subject to parent critique
No Board Member, Co-Director, or parent may challenge, dispute, or pressure either coach to reverse any coaching decision or family priority

This policy is in the bylaws. It's not a handshake agreement — it's a formal Board-level commitment that protects both coaches and sets the expectation for every family that joins this program.
